Scripture never stops to define fear, but it speaks of it in two ways that do not reduce to each other.

  • Fear comes upon a person. "Dread came upon me, and trembling, which made all my bones shake" (Job 4:14). "Fear and trembling come upon me" (Psalm 55:5). "Fear fell upon him" (Luke 1:12). In these verses the person is the one acted on.
  • The fear of God is a standing. It is required: "what does the Lord your God require of you, but to fear the Lord your God" (Deuteronomy 10:12). It is learned: "that they may hear and learn to fear the Lord your God" (Deuteronomy 31:12). It is given: "I will put the fear of me in their hearts, that they may not turn from me" (Jeremiah 32:40). It is owed: "If then I am a father, where is my honor? And if I am a master, where is my fear?" (Malachi 1:6).

One sentence holds both. At Sinai the people were afraid and stood far off, and Moses said: "Do not fear, for God has come to test you, that the fear of him may be before you, that you may not sin" (Exodus 20:20). He takes one fear away and sets another in its place, in the same breath.

The nearest the verses come to saying what fear is, is in John's letter: "There is no fear in love, but perfect love casts out fear. For fear has to do with punishment" (1 John 4:18). The verse before gives its setting: love is perfected "so that we may have confidence for the day of judgment" (4:17). It is said of fear before judgment. I do not take it as a definition of every fear.

The words

The English "fear" covers many Hebrew and Greek words, and one word can be rendered many ways.

  • Yārēʾ, "to fear". It is the ordinary word, used for fear of an enemy and for the fear of God. Turned round, it describes the one who is feared: God is "awesome" (Deuteronomy 10:17), and the wilderness is "great and terrifying" (Deuteronomy 1:19). It is also rendered as a way of acting: "One who is wise is cautious and turns away from evil" (Proverbs 14:16).
  • Paḥad, "dread". This is the dread that falls on a person. Once the same verb is used of joy: "your heart shall thrill and exult" (Isaiah 60:5).
  • Ḥārad, "to tremble". The body trembles with it. Yet Eli "was sitting on his seat by the road watching, for his heart trembled for the ark of God" (1 Samuel 4:13).
  • Rāgaz, "to tremble, be stirred". The earth quakes with it. The ESV also renders it "Be angry, and do not sin" (Psalm 4:4), "Do not quarrel on the way" (Genesis 45:24), and David "was deeply moved" and wept for Absalom (2 Samuel 18:33).
  • Môrāʾ, "fear, terror". This is the word of "Let him be your fear, and let him be your dread" (Isaiah 8:13), and of God's "great deeds of terror" in Egypt (Deuteronomy 4:34).
  • The terror and horror words. Māgôr, "terror", gives the name "Terror on Every Side" (Jeremiah 20:3). Šammâ, "horror, desolation", names a people as what others are horrified at: "I will make them a horror to all the kingdoms of the earth" (Jeremiah 24:9).
  • The Greek words. Phobos, "fear", and its verb carry most of the New Testament verses. The ESV renders the one word as terror, respect, reverence and awe: "rulers are not a terror to good conduct, but to bad"; "respect to whom respect is owed" (Romans 13:3, 7); "submitting to one another out of reverence for Christ" (Ephesians 5:21); "filled with awe" (Luke 5:26). With it go tromos, "trembling"; deilia, "cowardice"; eulabeia, "reverence"; and ekthambeō, "to be alarmed".

I read these words as naming a stirring that the verse and its situation then make into fear, awe, respect, anger, grief, care or joy. That is why one root can be rendered so many ways.

Fear that comes upon a person

Fear is felt in the heart and the body together.

  • "he was afraid, and his heart trembled greatly" (1 Samuel 28:5)
  • "My flesh trembles for fear of you" (Psalm 119:120)
  • "My heart is in anguish within me … Fear and trembling come upon me, and horror overwhelms me" (Psalm 55:4–5)
  • "fighting without and fear within" (2 Corinthians 7:5)

It has a cause outside, and often a reason inside. "I heard the sound of you in the garden, and I was afraid, because I was naked, and I hid myself" (Genesis 3:10). The sound was outside Adam. The reason was something he knew about himself.

The verses name what it rises at:

  • a report or a sight: "We have heard the report of it; our hands fall helpless; anguish has taken hold of us, pain as of a woman in labor" (Jeremiah 6:24). "When Saul and all Israel heard these words of the Philistine, they were dismayed and greatly afraid" (1 Samuel 17:11).
  • an arrival: the elders "came to meet him trembling and said, 'Do you come peaceably?'" (1 Samuel 16:4)
  • a discovery: "Isaac trembled very violently" (Genesis 27:33)
  • the night, and a spirit not clearly seen: "A spirit glided past my face; the hair of my flesh stood up. It stood still, but I could not discern its appearance" (Job 4:15–16)
  • a dream: "I saw a dream that made me afraid. As I lay in bed the fancies and the visions of my head alarmed me" (Daniel 4:5)
  • a mistaken seeing: the disciples saw Jesus walking on the sea "and said, 'It is a ghost!' and they cried out in fear" (Matthew 14:26). After the resurrection "they were startled and frightened and thought they saw a spirit" (Luke 24:37). Both times the answer was to be shown: "Take heart; it is I" (Matthew 14:27); "See my hands and my feet, that it is I myself. Touch me, and see" (Luke 24:39).

Terror can also come as an event, from outside.

  • It strikes: "I will mock when terror strikes you, when terror strikes you like a storm" (Proverbs 1:26–27). Wisdom is the speaker. The same speech says they "did not choose the fear of the Lord" (1:29). I read the passage as setting the fear of the Lord, not chosen, beside the terror that comes.
  • It comes where peace was looked for: "We looked for peace, but no good came; for a time of healing, but behold, terror" (Jeremiah 8:15).
  • It is given in the curse: "the Lord will give you there a trembling heart and failing eyes and a languishing soul" (Deuteronomy 28:65). The mind itself is struck: "The Lord will strike you with madness and blindness and confusion of mind" (Deuteronomy 28:28).
  • It can come where there is nothing to fear: "There they are, in great terror, where there is no terror!" (Psalm 53:5).

And it can stay, or come again. "For the thing that I fear comes upon me, and what I dread befalls me" (Job 3:25). "The sword that you fear shall overtake you there" (Jeremiah 42:16). Afterwards it is turned over in the mind: "Your heart will muse on the terror" (Isaiah 33:18).

What fear does

The verses follow fear into what the person does next. Some of these lead away from God and from others, and some lead toward them.

  • It hides and keeps away. "I was afraid, because I was naked, and I hid myself" (Genesis 3:10). At Sinai "the people were afraid and trembled, and they stood far off" (Exodus 20:18). Joseph of Arimathea was a disciple "secretly for fear of the Jews" (John 19:38). The disciples met "the doors being locked … for fear of the Jews" (John 20:19). Section 10.11 follows this.
  • It flees. Uriah "was afraid and fled and escaped to Egypt" (Jeremiah 26:21).
  • It silences. "Samuel was afraid to tell the vision to Eli" (1 Samuel 3:15). "Yet for fear of the Jews no one spoke openly of him" (John 7:13). "They did not understand the saying, and were afraid to ask him" (Mark 9:32). Section 10.5 follows this.
  • It bends speech and conduct. Sarah said she did not laugh, "for she was afraid" (Genesis 18:15). Saul: "I feared the people and obeyed their voice" (1 Samuel 15:24). At Antioch Peter "drew back and separated himself, fearing the circumcision party. And the rest of the Jews acted hypocritically along with him" (Galatians 2:12–13).
  • It holds back the hand, but not the wish. "The young man did not draw his sword, for he was afraid" (Judges 8:20). The chief priests were "seeking to arrest him", but "they feared the crowds" (Matthew 21:46). Herod "wanted to put him to death", but "he feared the people" (Matthew 14:5). The arrest is not made. The verses do not say the wish has changed.
  • It stops the will. Saul's armor-bearer "would not, for he feared greatly" (1 Samuel 31:4). "David was afraid of the Lord that day … So David was not willing to take the ark" (2 Samuel 6:9–10). Israel "would not go up" when "Our brothers have made our hearts melt" (Deuteronomy 1:26, 28). In David's case what he heard next moved him again (2 Samuel 6:12). 10.7 (Willing) follows the will.
  • It hardens into enmity. "Saul was even more afraid of David. So Saul was David's enemy continually" (1 Samuel 18:29).
  • It spreads. The fearful man is sent home "lest he make the heart of his fellows melt like his own" (Deuteronomy 20:8). "Great fear came upon all who heard of it" (Acts 5:5).
  • It turns to God. "When I am afraid, I put my trust in you" (Psalm 56:3). "Jehoshaphat was afraid and set his face to seek the Lord" (2 Chronicles 20:3). Peter, sinking: "Lord, save me" (Matthew 14:30).
  • It comes forward and tells the truth. The woman healed of her bleeding, "knowing what had happened to her, came in fear and trembling and fell down before him and told him the whole truth" (Mark 5:33). The jailer, "trembling with fear he fell down before Paul and Silas", asked, "Sirs, what must I do to be saved?" (Acts 16:29–30).
  • It glorifies God, or asks him to go. "When the crowds saw it, they were afraid, and they glorified God" (Matthew 9:8). But when the Gerasenes saw the healed man, they "asked him to depart from them, for they were seized with great fear" (Luke 8:37). Felix, hearing of "righteousness and self-control and the coming judgment", "was alarmed and said, 'Go away for the present'" (Acts 24:25).
  • It asks a question. "Who then is this, that even the wind and the sea obey him?" (Mark 4:41). "What then will this child be?" (Luke 1:66).

Fear before God

Before God's presence, his voice, his word and his acts, fear comes upon people, and the verses record it without softening.

  • "I am terrified at his presence; when I consider, I am in dread of him" (Job 23:15)
  • "At this also my heart trembles and leaps out of its place. Keep listening to the thunder of his voice" (Job 37:1–2)
  • "Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look at God" (Exodus 3:6). At the bush, "Moses trembled and did not dare to look" (Acts 7:32).
  • At Sinai, "so terrifying was the sight that Moses said, 'I tremble with fear'" (Hebrews 12:21)
  • Job names it as the dread of God's own terrors: "the terrors of God are arrayed against me" (Job 6:4)

The same happens before God's messengers. Zechariah "was troubled when he saw him, and fear fell upon him" (Luke 1:12). Cornelius "stared at him in terror and said, 'What is it, Lord?'" (Acts 10:4). At the tomb, "for fear of him the guards trembled and became like dead men" (Matthew 28:4).

And it happens before Jesus' acts. In the boat the disciples were first afraid of the storm: "Teacher, do you not care that we are perishing?" (Mark 4:38). Then he stilled it, and "they were filled with great fear and said to one another, 'Who then is this?'" (4:41). The same people, with the same word, are afraid twice, and the second fear is of him.

Jesus himself was distressed. In Gethsemane he "began to be greatly distressed and troubled" (Mark 14:33). Hebrews says he "offered up prayers and supplications, with loud cries and tears", and "was heard because of his reverence" (Hebrews 5:7). The word rendered "troubled" for Jesus is the word for the crowd that was "greatly amazed" and ran to greet him (Mark 9:15), and for the women who were "alarmed" at the tomb (Mark 16:5).

Fear before a holy act is not one response. The verses set two ends side by side:

  • glory and confession: "Fear seized them all, and they glorified God" (Luke 7:16); the centurion, "filled with awe", said, "Truly this was the Son of God!" (Matthew 27:54)
  • sending away: "Depart from me, for I am a sinful man, O Lord" (Luke 5:8); the Gerasenes (Luke 8:37); Felix (Acts 24:25)

Peter's plea is answered: "Do not be afraid; from now on you will be catching men" (Luke 5:10).

The fear of God

The same word is used for fearing God. Fear of a threat can make a person hide and run (above). Of this fear God says that it keeps a person from turning away: "that they may not turn from me" (Jeremiah 32:40).

It is a standing, and the verses say how it is held.

  • Commanded: "to fear the Lord your God, to walk in all his ways, to love him, to serve the Lord your God with all your heart and with all your soul" (Deuteronomy 10:12)
  • Learned, by hearing: "that they may hear and learn to fear the Lord your God" (Deuteronomy 31:12)
  • Given, in the heart: "I will put the fear of me in their hearts" (Jeremiah 32:40). God longs for it: "Oh that they had such a heart as this always, to fear me" (Deuteronomy 5:29).
  • Possible without the heart: "their hearts are far from me, and their fear of me is a commandment taught by men" (Isaiah 29:13)
  • Owed: "where is my fear?", said to priests "who despise my name" (Malachi 1:6)
  • A covenant: "It was a covenant of fear, and he feared me. He stood in awe of my name. True instruction was in his mouth, and no wrong was found on his lips" (Malachi 2:5–6)

The verses join it to:

  • turning from evil: "by the fear of the Lord one turns away from evil" (Proverbs 16:6); "The fear of the Lord is hatred of evil" (Proverbs 8:13)
  • knowledge: "The fear of the Lord is the beginning of knowledge" (Proverbs 1:7)
  • confidence: "In the fear of the Lord one has strong confidence" (Proverbs 14:26)
  • life: "a fountain of life" (Proverbs 14:27)

It sits with joy: "Serve the Lord with fear, and rejoice with trembling" (Psalm 2:11). It trembles, but at God's word: "this is the one to whom I will look: he who is humble and contrite in spirit and trembles at my word" (Isaiah 66:2).

In the New Testament the same standing is lived.

  • The church was "walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit" (Acts 9:31).
  • "Knowing the fear of the Lord, we persuade others" (2 Corinthians 5:11).
  • "Bringing holiness to completion in the fear of God" (2 Corinthians 7:1).
  • "In every nation anyone who fears him and does what is right is acceptable to him" (Acts 10:35).

In the Old Testament, "Fear and trembling come upon me" is a cry of distress (Psalm 55:5). In the New Testament the phrase "fear and trembling" is used in five verses, and in each it goes with coming near, not with running away. Two more verses join trembling to coming forward (Luke 8:47; Acts 16:29):

  • obedience: "work out your own salvation with fear and trembling, for it is God who works in you, both to will and to work for his good pleasure" (Philippians 2:12–13)
  • a sincere heart: "obey your earthly masters with fear and trembling, with a sincere heart, as you would Christ" (Ephesians 6:5)
  • receiving God's messenger: "how you received him with fear and trembling" (2 Corinthians 7:15)
  • weakness that rests on God's power: "I was with you in weakness and in fear and much trembling", "so that your faith might not rest in the wisdom of men but in the power of God" (1 Corinthians 2:3, 5)
  • telling the truth: Mark 5:33, Luke 8:47, Acts 16:29

Not every trembling does this. At the tomb "they went out and fled from the tomb, for trembling and astonishment had seized them" (Mark 16:8).

Fear of God is also called for as a guard, and set against pride. "So do not become proud, but fear. For if God did not spare the natural branches, neither will he spare you" (Romans 11:20–21). "Let us fear lest any of you should seem to have failed to reach it" (Hebrews 4:1).

And it stands with God as Father. Malachi puts both in one verse: a father to be honoured, a master to be feared (Malachi 1:6). Peter writes: "if you call on him as Father who judges impartially according to each one's deeds, conduct yourselves with fear throughout the time of your exile" (1 Peter 1:17). Paul names a different fear, the fear of a slave, and sets it against sonship: "you did not receive the spirit of slavery to fall back into fear, but you have received the Spirit of adoption as sons, by whom we cry, 'Abba! Father!'" (Romans 8:15). The verses name two fears here, by their own words. I set them side by side and do not join them.

Not to fear God is named as a lack. "There is no fear of God before their eyes" (Romans 3:18). The unjust judge "neither feared God nor respected man", and said so to himself (Luke 18:2, 4). On the cross, one criminal asked the other: "Do you not fear God, since you are under the same sentence of condemnation? And we indeed justly, for we are receiving the due reward of our deeds" (Luke 23:40–41).

"Fear not"

In the verses, fear of a threat is not argued away. It is met by someone, and a reason is given.

  • God's reason is mostly himself: "Fear not, Abram, I am your shield" (Genesis 15:1); "fear not, for I am with you" (Isaiah 41:10); "I have redeemed you" (Isaiah 43:1).
  • Jesus' reason is his presence, or the Father's care: "Take heart; it is I. Do not be afraid" (Matthew 14:27); "Fear not, little flock, for it is your Father's good pleasure to give you the kingdom" (Luke 12:32).
  • An angel's reason is the news brought: "Do not be afraid, Zechariah, for your prayer has been heard" (Luke 1:13); "Fear not, for behold, I bring you good news of great joy" (Luke 2:10).
  • Sometimes the reason is a task: "Do not be afraid, Paul; you must stand before Caesar" (Acts 27:24); "Do not be afraid; from now on you will be catching men" (Luke 5:10); "Do not be afraid, but go on speaking and do not be silent, for I am with you" (Acts 18:9–10).

Sometimes a hand comes with the word. "Jesus came and touched them, saying, 'Rise, and have no fear'" (Matthew 17:7). "He laid his right hand on me, saying, 'Fear not'" (Revelation 1:17). When Peter sank, "Jesus immediately reached out his hand and took hold of him" (Matthew 14:31).

The same words can be spoken to harm. Jael says "do not be afraid" to Sisera before the tent peg (Judges 4:18). What makes fear not true is who says it, and why.

A person can say it to himself: "When I am afraid, I put my trust in you. In God, whose word I praise, in God I trust; I shall not be afraid" (Psalm 56:3–4). "So we can confidently say, 'The Lord is my helper; I will not fear; what can man do to me?'" (Hebrews 13:6), after "I will never leave you nor forsake you" (13:5).

And peace is promised in the same terms: "I will give peace in the land, and you shall lie down, and none shall make you afraid" (Leviticus 26:6). The same phrase is also used of ruin, of deserted cities where flocks "will lie down, and none will make them afraid" (Isaiah 17:2).

Fear set against faith and love

Jesus puts fear and faith against each other by asking questions. "Why are you so afraid? Have you still no faith?" (Mark 4:40). "Do not fear, only believe" (Mark 5:36). Hebrews states the reverse: "By faith he left Egypt, not being afraid of the anger of the king, for he endured as seeing him who is invisible" (Hebrews 11:27).

Proverbs sets fear of people against trust: "The fear of man lays a snare, but whoever trusts in the Lord is safe" (Proverbs 29:25).

John sets fear against love, in view of judgment: "perfect love casts out fear. For fear has to do with punishment, and whoever fears has not been perfected in love" (1 John 4:18).

Paul names a spirit God did not give: "God gave us a spirit not of fear but of power and love and self-control". He says it so that Timothy will "not be ashamed of the testimony about our Lord" and will "share in suffering for the gospel by the power of God" (2 Timothy 1:7–8).

Fear of death is named as a bondage that Christ came to end: he came to "deliver all those who through fear of death were subject to lifelong slavery" (Hebrews 2:15). Chapter 2 follows it.

The word for cowardice stands in two places. In the boat it is Jesus' question, "Why are you afraid, O you of little faith?" (Matthew 8:26). At the end, "the cowardly" head the list of those whose portion is "the second death" (Revelation 21:8).

Fear between people

Fear of other people takes several forms in the verses.

  • Fear of man. "who are you that you are afraid of man who dies" (Isaiah 51:12). "do not fear those who kill the body but cannot kill the soul" (Matthew 10:28).
  • Fear for another. Eli trembled for the ark (1 Samuel 4:13). Jeremiah grieved for his people: "For the wound of the daughter of my people is my heart wounded; I mourn, and dismay has taken hold on me" (Jeremiah 8:21). Paul: "I am afraid that as the serpent deceived Eve by his cunning, your thoughts will be led astray from a sincere and pure devotion to Christ" (2 Corinthians 11:3).
  • Fear used as a tool. "he was hired, that I should be afraid and act in this way and sin" (Nehemiah 6:13).
  • Respect owed. The same word as fear is rendered "respect" toward people. "respect to whom respect is owed, honor to whom honor is owed" (Romans 13:7). "Servants, be subject to your masters with all respect, not only to the good and gentle but also to the unjust" (1 Peter 2:18). Toward parents: "Every one of you shall revere his mother and his father" (Leviticus 19:3).
  • Respect and no fear, two verses apart. "Have no fear of them, nor be troubled, but in your hearts honor Christ the Lord as holy, always being prepared to make a defense to anyone who asks you for a reason for the hope that is in you; yet do it with gentleness and respect" (1 Peter 3:14–15). In Greek, "fear" and "respect" are the same word. The verse does not say whether the respect is toward the one who asks or toward God.
  • Being feared. Being feared works on the one who is feared. "The horror you inspire has deceived you, and the pride of your heart" (Jeremiah 49:16).

Paul's account of the ruler holds fear and respect together, and ties them to conduct: "rulers are not a terror to good conduct, but to bad. Would you have no fear of the one who is in authority? Then do what is good, and you will receive his approval" (Romans 13:3).

Dread laid on others

Fear does not stay with single persons. God lays dread on peoples and on creatures, and the verses say what it led to.

  • "The fear of you and the dread of you shall be upon every beast of the earth" (Genesis 9:2).
  • "a terror from God fell upon the cities that were around them, so that they did not pursue the sons of Jacob" (Genesis 35:5).
  • "Put them in fear, O Lord! Let the nations know that they are but men!" (Psalm 9:20).

The same dread came to two peoples and ended in opposite ways.

  • Rahab: "as soon as we heard it, our hearts melted, and there was no spirit left in any man because of you, for the Lord your God, he is God in the heavens above and on the earth beneath" (Joshua 2:11).
  • Moab: "Moab was in great dread of the people, because they were many", and Balak sent for Balaam: "Come now, curse this people for me, since they are too mighty for me" (Numbers 22:3, 6).

A people can itself be made a horror to others. "I will make them a horror to all the kingdoms of the earth, to be a reproach, a byword, a taunt, and a curse" (Jeremiah 24:9). "Everyone who passes by it will be horrified and will hiss because of all its wounds" (Jeremiah 19:8). The feeling is in those who pass by. The verses give the reasons in the people's own deeds: "The Lord could no longer bear your evil deeds" (Jeremiah 44:22). The same root, held by the prophet, is his own grief for them: "dismay has taken hold on me" (Jeremiah 8:21).

One phrase passes through many mouths. Jeremiah gave it as a name: "The Lord does not call your name Pashhur, but Terror on Every Side" (Jeremiah 20:3). Then he heard it whispered against himself: "For I hear many whispering. Terror is on every side! 'Denounce him! Let us denounce him!' say all my close friends" (20:10). He answered: "But the Lord is with me as a dread warrior" (20:11). A psalmist heard "the whispering of many" and "terror on every side" (Psalm 31:13), and answered in his own words: "But I trust in you, O Lord; I say, 'You are my God'" (31:14).

Job's friends say that terrors chase the wicked: "Terrors frighten him on every side, and chase him at his heels" (Job 18:11). They say it of Job. God's own verdict stands over them: "you have not spoken of me what is right, as my servant Job has" (Job 42:7).

Other parties

Each party that comes into fear's verses does so in its own way.

  • God lays dread (Genesis 35:5). He speaks fear not with himself as its ground. His acts are named by the terror word: "great deeds of terror, all of which the Lord your God did for you in Egypt before your eyes", "that you might know that the Lord is God" (Deuteronomy 4:34–35). Once he speaks of holding back "had I not feared provocation by the enemy" (Deuteronomy 32:27), and the reason he gives is what the enemy would say.
  • Job names God's terrors as borne in his spirit: "the arrows of the Almighty are in me; my spirit drinks their poison; the terrors of God are arrayed against me" (Job 6:4).
  • The Holy Spirit is named with the fear of the Lord: "walking in the fear of the Lord and in the comfort of the Holy Spirit" (Acts 9:31). On the shoot of Jesse rests "the Spirit of knowledge and the fear of the Lord" (Isaiah 11:2).
  • Angels bring fear and speak against it (Luke 1:12–13; 2:9–10).
  • Other spirits. A spirit unseen raises dread in the night (Job 4:15). Demons believe and shudder: "You believe that God is one; you do well. Even the demons believe—and shudder!" (James 2:19).
  • The physical world trembles before God in the words used of people. "When the waters saw you, O God, when the waters saw you, they were afraid; indeed, the deep trembled" (Psalm 77:16). The waters also go over a person as God's: "Why are you cast down, O my soul … all your breakers and your waves have gone over me" (Psalm 42:5, 7).
  • Creatures are made "without fear" (Job 41:33), and dread is laid on them (Genesis 9:2).
  • A loved one is described by the dread word. The beloved is "awesome as an army with banners" (Song of Songs 6:4).

What changes it

The verses show fear changing character, and they point to what changes it.

  • What it is turned toward. At the sea Israel "feared greatly" as Egypt marched, and after the crossing "the people feared the Lord, and they believed in the Lord" (Exodus 14:10, 31). In between, fear spoke as a preference: "it would have been better for us to serve the Egyptians than to die in the wilderness" (14:12; 10.6, Shaped by fear). In the boat, fear of the storm became fear of the one who stilled it (Mark 4:41). In these cases the change came after God acted.
  • One fear set in place of another, by the same speaker. "Do not fear, for God has come to test you, that the fear of him may be before you" (Exodus 20:20). "do not fear what they fear … Let him be your fear" (Isaiah 8:12–13). "Rather fear him who can destroy both soul and body in hell", and then, "Fear not, therefore; you are of more value than many sparrows" (Matthew 10:28, 31).
  • Who speaks to it, and why. God's "for I am with you" (Isaiah 41:10), and Jael's "do not be afraid" (Judges 4:18).
  • Who meets it. "When justice is done, it is a joy to the righteous but terror to evildoers" (Proverbs 21:15). One scroll was read aloud: the officials "turned one to another in fear", and "neither the king nor any of his servants who heard all these words was afraid" (Jeremiah 36:16, 24). At the signs of the end, "people fainting with fear", and to the disciples, "straighten up and raise your heads, because your redemption is drawing near" (Luke 21:26, 28).
  • One's own conduct. "rulers are not a terror to good conduct, but to bad" (Romans 13:3). "I also will choose harsh treatment for them and bring their fears upon them, because when I called, no one answered" (Isaiah 66:4). The same passage opens with the one God looks to, who "trembles at my word" (66:2). I read the passage as setting that trembling beside "their fears".
  • Pride. "So do not become proud, but fear" (Romans 11:20). "The horror you inspire has deceived you, and the pride of your heart" (Jeremiah 49:16).
  • Where the heart is. Fear "taught by men" while "their hearts are far from me", against fear put "in their hearts" (Isaiah 29:13; Jeremiah 32:40).
  • Faith, trust and love (the section above).
  • Joy or silence. The women left the tomb "with fear and great joy, and ran to tell his disciples" (Matthew 28:8). In Mark's account "they said nothing to anyone, for they were afraid" (Mark 16:8). The two accounts stand side by side.
  • The same root, rendered as another movement. Rāgaz, "to tremble", is also anger and grief (Psalm 4:4; 2 Samuel 18:33). Paḥad, "dread", is also "thrill" (Isaiah 60:5). Phobos, "fear", is also "respect" (Romans 13:7).

Chapter 11 (§4) gathers what changes the character of a word across all the activities. These are fear's part in it.

Connections

Fear touches many of the activities in this Part. The verses join it to the others in their own wording: by paired commands, by "but", by "for" and "so that", and by sharing a root.

  • The heart (Chapter 4): fear is felt there, given there, and kept out of it: "His heart is steady; he will not be afraid" (Psalm 112:8).
  • Losing heart and courage: "Do not fear or be dismayed" (Deuteronomy 31:8); "Be strong and courageous. Do not fear" (Deuteronomy 31:6).
  • Knowing and hearing (10.2): the fear of the Lord is "the beginning of knowledge" (Proverbs 1:7). It is learned by hearing, and it spreads to "all who heard of it" (Acts 5:5).
  • Speaking (10.5): fear silences, bends speech, and is told to "go on speaking" (Acts 18:9).
  • Choosing (10.7): fear holds back the hand.
  • Relating to God (10.9): the fear of God; "Fear not"; trust.
  • Relating to others (10.10): fear of man; fear for another; respect owed.
  • Hiding and disclosing (10.11): fear hides, and fear also comes forward and tells "the whole truth" (Mark 5:33).
  • Feeling (10.1): fear sits beside anger and grief. One root is shared with both (rāgaz).
  • Love, joy and peace: commanded with love (Deuteronomy 10:12) and cast out by it (1 John 4:18); joined with joy (Psalm 2:11); "none shall make you afraid" with peace (Leviticus 26:6).
  • Going wrong (Chapter 12): fear that leads to lying and hypocrisy; no fear of God.
  • Under God's anger (Chapter 13): dread laid on, a horror made, terror as event.
  • Made new (Chapter 14): fear given in the heart; the spirit not of fear.
  • Life and death (Chapter 2): the fear of death; the fear of the Lord as "a fountain of life" (Proverbs 14:27).
