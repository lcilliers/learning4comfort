The Presence of God

7. Trembling before him

In this chapter

  1. Creation shakes
  2. The idols and the proud
  3. Who can stand?
  4. Silence before him
  5. The fear that keeps from sin
  6. Before the throne

God's presence is joy for those who walk before him (Chapter 6). It is also, in many verses, overwhelming. Creation shakes before him, the proud hide, and every person will one day stand before his throne. These verses belong to the same subject. They show that the presence of God is the presence of the holy one, the judge of all the earth.

Creation shakes

When God comes, the earth responds. "Tremble, O earth, at the presence of the Lord, at the presence of the God of Jacob" (Psalm 114:7). The song of Deborah remembers Sinai: "The mountains quaked before the Lord, even Sinai before the Lord, the God of Israel" (Judges 5:5). The psalms and prophets use the same pictures again and again:

  • "The mountains melt like wax before the Lord, before the Lord of all the earth" (Psalm 97:5)
  • "The mountains quake before him; the hills melt; the earth heaves before him, the world and all who dwell in it" (Nahum 1:5)
  • "the earth quaked, the heavens poured down rain, before God, the One of Sinai" (Psalm 68:8)
  • the creatures of sea, sky and field, "and all the people who are on the face of the earth, shall quake at my presence" (Ezekiel 38:20)

This is not described as a disaster in itself. It is what happens when the creator comes near his creation. Isaiah even prays for it: "Oh that you would rend the heavens and come down, that the mountains might quake at your presence" (Isaiah 64:1), "that the nations might tremble at your presence!" (Isaiah 64:2), remembering how "you came down, the mountains quaked at your presence" (Isaiah 64:3).

The idols and the proud

What shakes most of all is everything set up against God. "The idols of Egypt will tremble at his presence, and the heart of the Egyptians will melt within them" (Isaiah 19:1). "God shall arise, his enemies shall be scattered; and those who hate him shall flee before him!" (Psalm 68:1). In the same psalm the two responses stand side by side: "as wax melts before fire, so the wicked shall perish before God! But the righteous shall be glad; they shall exult before God" (Psalm 68:2–3).

Isaiah describes the day when human pride gives way: "Enter into the rock and hide in the dust from before the terror of the Lord, and from the splendor of his majesty" (Isaiah 2:10). Revelation gives the same scene at the end: people "calling to the mountains and rocks, 'Fall on us and hide us from the face of him who is seated on the throne, and from the wrath of the Lamb'" (Revelation 6:16). It is the opposite of Psalm 27. The face that the believer seeks is the face from which the unrepentant try to hide.

Who can stand?

Again and again the verses ask whether anyone can stand in God's presence. The men of Beth-shemesh asked it (1 Samuel 6:20, in Chapter 5). Nahum asks it of God's anger: "Who can stand before his indignation? Who can endure the heat of his anger?" (Nahum 1:6). Ezra answers it in his confession for the people: "Behold, we are before you in our guilt, for none can stand before you because of this" (Ezra 9:15).

Job, who knew he was innocent of the charges against him, still says, "Therefore I am terrified at his presence; when I consider, I am in dread of him" (Job 23:15). God's presence is not frightening only to the wicked. Even the upright feel its weight.

Isaiah felt it most sharply. When he saw the Lord in the temple, high and lifted up, and heard the seraphim call him holy, his first words were of his own unworthiness, and he was cleansed before he was sent (Isaiah 6). The awe of God's holiness brings with it a sense of personal unfitness and a need to be made clean in his sight.

One psalm asks the question in the language of worship and gives an answer:

Who shall ascend the hill of the Lord? And who shall stand in his holy place? He who has clean hands and a pure heart, who does not lift up his soul to what is false, and does not swear deceitfully. (Psalm 24:3–4)

The answer builds on the priests' teaching that purity is needed to come near, but it goes further. Clean hands are not enough without a pure heart: to stand in his presence takes integrity and single-minded devotion to God. The prophets pressed the same point against worship that had become empty. Sacrifice without righteous living and inward cleansing was worthless (Isaiah 1:10–20; Jeremiah 7:1–27). The holiness of God's presence demands justice.

The New Testament gives the same verdict: "by works of the law no human being will be justified in his sight" (Romans 3:20), and God has acted "so that no human being might boast in the presence of God" (1 Corinthians 1:29). No one stands before God by right.

Silence before him

One response is simply silence. "The Lord is in his holy temple; let all the earth keep silence before him" (Habakkuk 2:20). "Be silent before the Lord God! For the day of the Lord is near" (Zephaniah 1:7). Silence is not emptiness here. It is what is fitting when the creature is before the creator, and the guilty before the judge.

The fear that keeps from sin

Fear in God's presence is not meant to drive people away from him. At Sinai, Moses told the frightened people, "Do not fear, for God has come to test you, that the fear of him may be before you, that you may not sin" (Exodus 20:20). The Preacher says that God has done all he does "so that people fear before him" (Ecclesiastes 3:14), and that "it will be well with those who fear God, because they fear before him" (Ecclesiastes 8:12). Rightly understood, the fear of his presence is part of walking before him.

Before the throne

The verses look ahead to a final standing before God. Daniel saw it: "A stream of fire issued and came out from before him; a thousand thousands served him, and ten thousand times ten thousand stood before him; the court sat in judgment, and the books were opened" (Daniel 7:10). John saw it too: "Then I saw a great white throne and him who was seated on it. From his presence earth and sky fled away, and no place was found for them. And I saw the dead, great and small, standing before the throne, and books were opened" (Revelation 20:11–12).

For those who reject him, the end is to be shut out: "They will suffer the punishment of eternal destruction, away from the presence of the Lord and from the glory of his might" (2 Thessalonians 1:9). This is the darkest use of the word "presence" in the study. It shows what the presence of God is worth. To be away from it is the final loss.

Yet even the coming of the judge is a cause of joy for creation: "Then shall the trees of the forest sing for joy before the Lord, for he comes to judge the earth" (1 Chronicles 16:33), and "He will judge the world in righteousness, and the peoples in his faithfulness" (Psalm 96:13). Chapter 11 returns to that hope.
