The Presence of God

6. Living before God

In this chapter

  1. Walking before God
  2. Eating and rejoicing before him
  3. Coming to worship
  4. Prayer poured out
  5. Humbling oneself before him
  6. Standing before him to serve
  7. Known before him

The largest group of verses gathered for this study use one small phrase: "before the Lord" or "before God". In Hebrew it is "to the face of" the Lord. It is used of sacrifice, of worship and feasting, of prayer, of walking through life, and of standing to serve. Taken together, these verses show that God's presence was not only a place to visit. It was the setting of a whole life.

Walking before God

God's first word to Abraham in the covenant chapter is, "I am God Almighty; walk before me, and be blameless" (Genesis 17:1). Abraham's servant later describes his master as one who lives that way: "The Lord, before whom I have walked, will send his angel with you" (Genesis 24:40). Jacob blesses his grandsons in the name of "The God before whom my fathers Abraham and Isaac walked, the God who has been my shepherd all my life long to this day" (Genesis 48:15).

To walk before God is to live the whole of life in his sight, knowing that he sees and caring for what he thinks. Solomon praises God for "showing steadfast love to your servants who walk before you with all their heart" (1 Kings 8:23). Hezekiah, facing death, prays, "remember how I have walked before you in faithfulness and with a whole heart" (2 Kings 20:3). The psalmist, rescued from death, says, "I will walk before the Lord in the land of the living" (Psalm 116:9), and "you have delivered my soul from death ... that I may walk before God in the light of life" (Psalm 56:13). In the New Testament, Zechariah and Elizabeth "were both righteous before God, walking blamelessly in all the commandments and statutes of the Lord" (Luke 1:6).

Eating and rejoicing before him

Much of what was done "before the Lord" was joyful. At the place God chose, "there you shall eat before the Lord your God, and you shall rejoice, you and your households, in all that you undertake" (Deuteronomy 12:7). At the Feast of Booths, "you shall rejoice before the Lord your God seven days" (Leviticus 23:40). When Solomon was made king, "they ate and drank before the Lord on that day with great gladness" (1 Chronicles 29:22). When the ark came to Jerusalem, "David danced before the Lord with all his might" (2 Samuel 6:14), and to Michal's scorn he answered, "I will celebrate before the Lord" (2 Samuel 6:21).

The presence of God is the place of joy. "In your presence there is fullness of joy; at your right hand are pleasures forevermore" (Psalm 16:11). The king is "glad with the joy of your presence" (Psalm 21:6). Peter quotes Psalm 16 of the risen Christ: "you will make me full of gladness with your presence" (Acts 2:28).

Coming to worship

The psalms invite the people into God's presence: "Let us come into his presence with thanksgiving; let us make a joyful noise to him with songs of praise!" (Psalm 95:2). "Serve the Lord with gladness! Come into his presence with singing!" (Psalm 100:2). Joy and reverence go together: "Oh come, let us worship and bow down; let us kneel before the Lord, our Maker!" (Psalm 95:6), and "Worship the Lord in the splendor of holiness; tremble before him, all the earth!" (Psalm 96:9).

The invitation widens to the nations. "All the families of the nations shall worship before you" (Psalm 22:27). "All the nations you have made shall come and worship before you, O Lord, and shall glorify your name" (Psalm 86:9). Isaiah ends his book with it: "all flesh shall come to worship before me, declares the Lord" (Isaiah 66:23).

Prayer poured out

To pray is to come before God. Hannah, misjudged by the priest, explains, "I have been pouring out my soul before the Lord" (1 Samuel 1:15). The psalm says the same to everyone: "Trust in him at all times, O people; pour out your heart before him; God is a refuge for us" (Psalm 62:8). In the ruins of Jerusalem: "Pour out your heart like water before the presence of the Lord!" (Lamentations 2:19).

Prayer before God can be bold and concrete. When Hezekiah received the enemy's threatening letter, he "went up to the house of the Lord and spread it before the Lord" (2 Kings 19:14). David, given a promise, "went in and sat before the Lord" and asked, "Who am I, O Lord God, and what is my house, that you have brought me thus far?" (2 Samuel 7:18). Nehemiah prays "before you day and night" (Nehemiah 1:6), and the psalmist cries "day and night before you" (Psalm 88:1). And the Preacher adds a caution: "nor let your heart be hasty to utter a word before God, for God is in heaven and you are on earth. Therefore let your words be few" (Ecclesiastes 5:2).

Humbling oneself before him

To come before God truly is to come low. God answers Josiah "because your heart was penitent, and you humbled yourself before the Lord" (2 Kings 22:19), and "you have humbled yourself before me and have torn your clothes and wept before me, I also have heard you" (2 Chronicles 34:27). James repeats the pattern in one line: "Humble yourselves before the Lord, and he will exalt you" (James 4:10).

Standing before him to serve

Some were set apart to stand in God's presence for the people. The Levites were chosen "to stand before the Lord to minister to him and to bless in his name" (Deuteronomy 10:8). Hezekiah reminded the priests, "the Lord has chosen you to stand in his presence, to minister to him" (2 Chronicles 29:11). The high priest carried the people with him: Aaron "shall bear the names of the sons of Israel in the breastpiece of judgment on his heart, when he goes into the Holy Place, to bring them to regular remembrance before the Lord" (Exodus 28:29).

The prophet stood there too. To Jeremiah, worn down, God said, "If you return, I will restore you, and you shall stand before me. If you utter what is precious, and not what is worthless, you shall be as my mouth" (Jeremiah 15:19). The angel who announced John's birth introduced himself the same way: "I am Gabriel. I stand in the presence of God, and I was sent to speak to you" (Luke 1:19). To stand before God is to be sent from him.

Known before him

Living before God also means being known by him, in mercy and in judgment. Nothing is overlooked: "not one of them is forgotten before God" (Luke 12:6), and "a book of remembrance was written before him of those who feared the Lord and esteemed his name" (Malachi 3:16). Nothing is hidden: "You have set our iniquities before you, our secret sins in the light of your presence" (Psalm 90:8). So the psalmist prays, "Let the words of my mouth and the meditation of my heart be acceptable in your sight, O Lord, my rock and my redeemer" (Psalm 19:14). He can also say, "I have set the Lord always before me; because he is at my right hand, I shall not be shaken" (Psalm 16:8).

For the person who lives this way, God's presence is not something to be visited now and then, but the ground they stand on: "you have upheld me because of my integrity, and set me in your presence forever" (Psalm 41:12). The command to "seek his presence continually" (1 Chronicles 16:11; Psalm 105:4) belongs here. It is not a call to find a God who is far away. It is a call to live, all the time, turned toward the God who is near.
