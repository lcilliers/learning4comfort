The Presence of God

2. The words for presence

In this chapter

  1. In the Old Testament
  2. In the New Testament
  3. What the words show

Neither Hebrew nor Greek has one word that always means "presence". The idea is carried by several words, each with its own picture. Knowing them helps to see what a verse is saying.

In the Old Testament

  • Pānîm, "face". The most common way of speaking of God's presence is to speak of his face. It is the ordinary word for a person's face, but it is used for presence itself. "My presence will go with you, and I will give you rest" (Exodus 33:14) is, word for word, "my face will go". The preposition built on it, "before" (literally "to the face of"), is behind most of the verses that speak of standing, walking, eating or praying "before the Lord".
  • Šākan, "to dwell", and miškān, "dwelling". God settles and dwells among his people. The tabernacle is his miškān, his dwelling place. Later Jewish writers took the word shekinah from this root as a name for God's glory dwelling in a place.
  • Miqdāš, "sanctuary". A holy place, set apart because God dwells there. The tabernacle and the temple are both called by this name.
  • Kābôd, "glory". The root means weight or heaviness. Used of God, his glory is his presence made manifest, often in light, fire or cloud. "The glory of the Lord" is almost a technical phrase for God's visible presence among his people.
  • Šēm, "name". God's name stands for God himself. He makes his name "dwell" in the place he chooses (Deuteronomy 14:23), and where his name is, he is.
  • Hālak, "to walk, to go". God is present when he walks among his people (Deuteronomy 23:14) or goes before them.
  • ʿĀbar, "to pass by". God makes himself known by passing by: before Moses (Exodus 34:6) and before Elijah (1 Kings 19:11).
  • Rûaḥ, "spirit". The Spirit of God comes upon people and is God's own presence with them. "Cast me not away from your presence, and take not your Holy Spirit from me" (Psalm 51:11) sets the two side by side.

In the New Testament

  • Prosōpon, "face". Like the Hebrew word, it means a face and is used for presence. It is the word in "then face to face" (1 Corinthians 13:12) and "they will see his face" (Revelation 22:4).
  • Enōpion, "before, in the sight of". It is used for being "before God" or "in the sight of God", often in a solemn sense: "In what I am writing to you, before God, I do not lie!" (Galatians 1:20).
  • Skēnē, "tent, tabernacle", and skēnoō, "to dwell". The Greek word for the tabernacle and the verb from it. John uses the verb when he says that the Word became flesh and dwelt among us (John 1:14), as if God pitched his tent among men. Revelation 21:3 uses both of the end, when God's dwelling is with man.
  • Parousia, "presence, coming". The word means being present, or arriving. It is used of ordinary visits, "the coming of Titus" (2 Corinthians 7:6), but above all of the coming of Christ at the end of the age.
  • Doxa, "glory". The Greek word that translates kābôd. In the New Testament it is rarely used for a visible appearance of God, because the glory is now seen in Jesus Christ: "the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Corinthians 4:6).

What the words show

Three things stand out. First, presence is personal. It is spoken of as a face, a name and a walking companion, not as a force. Second, presence is relational. Most of the verses are about being before God, coming to him, being sent away from him or being welcomed into his sight. Third, the words move. Old Testament words for a tent and a glory reappear in the New Testament, but now they describe a person, Jesus Christ, and then a people in whom God's Spirit lives.
