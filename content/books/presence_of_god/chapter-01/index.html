The Presence of God

1. Everywhere, and in one place

In this chapter

  1. Present everywhere
  2. Present in a particular place
  3. A hidden God who makes himself known
  4. Holding the two together

Scripture speaks of God's presence on two levels, and both are needed to read it well.

Present everywhere

At the broadest level, God is not bound to any place. No one can go where he is not:

Where shall I go from your Spirit? Or where shall I flee from your presence? (Psalm 139:7)

Nothing is out of his sight. "No creature is hidden from his sight, but all are naked and exposed to the eyes of him to whom we must give account" (Hebrews 4:13). Even the realm of the dead is open to him: "Sheol is naked before God, and Abaddon has no covering" (Job 26:6). Proverbs draws the conclusion: "Sheol and Abaddon lie open before the Lord; how much more the hearts of the children of man!" (Proverbs 15:11).

This is what theologians call God's omnipresence. It does not mean that God takes up a great deal of space, or that he is spread out thinly across the universe. It means that he is not limited by space at all. He made it. He is present to all places and all times because he is confined to none.

In its own setting this was a challenge. The gods of the nations around Israel were thought of as gods of a territory, with power that stopped at a border. Israel's God has no border. When Jonah "rose to flee to Tarshish from the presence of the Lord" (Jonah 1:3), the story shows that there was nowhere to go. The sailors knew "that he was fleeing from the presence of the Lord" (Jonah 1:10), and the Lord met him at sea.

Present in a particular place

Yet Scripture also speaks, again and again, of God being present in one place more than another, for a people, and with a purpose. He "walks in the midst of your camp, to deliver you" (Deuteronomy 23:14). He is "in your midst, a great and awesome God" (Deuteronomy 7:21). He chooses a place "to make his name dwell there" (Deuteronomy 16:11). Of the temple he says, "I have consecrated this house that you have built, by putting my name there forever. My eyes and my heart will be there for all time" (1 Kings 9:3).

This is not a contradiction of the first level. God who is everywhere freely chooses to be present in a particular way, in a particular place, to bless and to be known. The tabernacle and the temple are the Old Testament's chief examples. At the temple's dedication Solomon himself says that heaven cannot contain God, much less a house built by hands (1 Kings 8:27), and in the same prayer asks that God's eyes be open toward that house night and day.

The particular presence can also be withdrawn. When Israel set out without him, Moses warned, "Do not go up, for the Lord is not among you" (Numbers 14:42). The Lord said the same: "I am not in your midst, lest you be defeated before your enemies" (Deuteronomy 1:42). God had not ceased to be everywhere. He had ceased to be with them in the way that mattered for the battle.

A hidden God who makes himself known

Because God is spirit and cannot be seen, every means he uses to show his presence falls short of him. In that sense the God of the Bible is a hidden God. But he is a hidden God who makes himself known. He shows himself in the great forces of nature: fire, wind and earthquake (1 Kings 19:11–13; Chapter 4). He appears in human form, as when three visitors came to Abraham (Genesis 18) and when Jacob wrestled through the night (Genesis 32:22–32). He sends the angel of his presence (Chapter 4). The means God chooses hold together two things that seem opposed: that he is far above his creation, and that he comes near to it.

Holding the two together

Most of what follows is about the second level: God's presence as he makes it known, gives it, hides it and promises it. But the first level stays underneath. It is why the particular presence is always a gift and never a possession. It is also why, in the New Testament, the move of God's presence from a building to a person, and then to a people, does not make any other place less his. Every place is already in his sight.

The Old Testament lays out the particular presence in pictures and places: a face, a cloud, a fire, a tent, a house. The New Testament speaks of it in Christ, in the Spirit and in the church, and looks ahead to a day when God's people will see his face.
