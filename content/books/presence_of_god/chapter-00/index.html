The Presence of God

Introduction

In this chapter

  1. What the study is built from
  2. How it is put together

This is a short study of one question: what does Scripture mean when it speaks of the presence of God?

The question sounds simple. The verses show that it is not. God is present everywhere: "Where shall I go from your Spirit? Or where shall I flee from your presence?" (Psalm 139:7). Yet the same Scriptures speak of God coming down, dwelling in one place, going before a people, hiding his face, and one day being seen face to face. A person can be "cast out" from his presence (2 Kings 24:20). A person can be told to "seek his presence continually" (1 Chronicles 16:11). Both cannot mean the same thing by "presence".

What the study is built from

The study began with a set of questions about the presence of God, put to a research assistant in the Logos Bible software, and the answers it gave from standard reference works: theological wordbooks, dictionaries of biblical theology and a survey of theology. Those answers gave the shape of the subject: the words Scripture uses, the difference between the Old and New Testaments, and the question whether God's indwelling leaves anything still to seek.

Behind them stands a much larger body of material: about 2,400 verses gathered by searching the English Bible for the words that carry the idea of presence: "presence", "face", "before" and "in the sight of". Many of those verses turned out to be about something else, the face of the ground or a man standing before a king. The rest were read for what they say about God's presence. They are the substance of this study.

How it is put together

Chapter 1 sets out the two sides of the subject: God present everywhere, and God present in a particular place for a particular people. Chapter 2 gives the main Hebrew and Greek words. Chapters 3 to 8 follow the Old Testament's ways of speaking: the face of God, the signs of cloud, fire and glory, the dwelling among his people, life lived before him, trembling before him, and being driven from his presence and brought back. Chapters 9 to 11 follow the New Testament: God present in Christ and by the Spirit, the seeking that still goes on, and the hope of seeing his face. Chapter 12 lists the sources.

Every quotation of Scripture is from the English Standard Version, taken from the verses gathered for the study. Some central passages were not among those verses, because they do not use the search words. They are referred to by their reference, and their content is described, but they are not quoted.
