The Presence of God

3. The face of God

In this chapter

  1. Seen, and not seen
  2. A face that shines
  3. A face that is hidden
  4. A face set against
  5. A face sought
  6. A face beheld

The Old Testament speaks of God's presence most often by speaking of his face. The face is where a person turns toward you, where you see whether they are pleased or displeased, and where you are known. So the verses speak of God's face being seen, shining, hidden, set against, sought, and one day beheld.

Seen, and not seen

Some are said to have met God face to face. Jacob named the place Peniel, "For I have seen God face to face, and yet my life has been delivered" (Genesis 32:30). Gideon cried out, "Alas, O Lord God! For now I have seen the angel of the Lord face to face" (Judges 6:22). Of Moses the verses are strongest: "Thus the Lord used to speak to Moses face to face, as a man speaks to his friend" (Exodus 33:11), and "there has not arisen a prophet since in Israel like Moses, whom the Lord knew face to face" (Deuteronomy 34:10). The whole people heard him so: "The Lord spoke with you face to face at the mountain, out of the midst of the fire" (Deuteronomy 5:4). The nations heard of it too: "For you, O Lord, are seen face to face, and your cloud stands over them" (Numbers 14:14).

Yet in the same chapter as Moses' friendship, God says, "you cannot see my face, for man shall not see me and live" (Exodus 33:20), and "you shall see my back, but my face shall not be seen" (Exodus 33:23). At the burning bush "Moses hid his face, for he was afraid to look at God" (Exodus 3:6).

Both are true. "Face to face" describes a meeting that is direct, personal and close: God speaks, and Moses hears and answers, as one friend to another. It does not mean that a human being can look on God as he is. The nearness is real, and so is the distance. What Moses was given instead of seeing God's face was to hear his name: "I will make all my goodness pass before you and will proclaim before you my name 'The Lord'" (Exodus 33:19). When God passed by, what he proclaimed was his character: "The Lord, the Lord, a God merciful and gracious, slow to anger, and abounding in steadfast love and faithfulness" (Exodus 34:6).

A face that shines

When God's face shines on someone, it means favour, grace and salvation. The priestly blessing asks for it: "the Lord make his face to shine upon you and be gracious to you" (Numbers 6:25). The psalms ask it again and again:

  • "Make your face shine on your servant; save me in your steadfast love!" (Psalm 31:16)
  • "May God be gracious to us and bless us and make his face to shine upon us" (Psalm 67:1)
  • "Restore us, O God; let your face shine, that we may be saved!" (Psalm 80:3, and again in 80:7 and 80:19)
  • "Make your face shine upon your servant, and teach me your statutes" (Psalm 119:135)

Daniel asks it for a ruined place: "for your own sake, O Lord, make your face to shine upon your sanctuary, which is desolate" (Daniel 9:17). A shining face is God turned toward a person in kindness.

A face that is hidden

The opposite is the hidden face. It is the Old Testament's way of speaking of God's presence withdrawn, so that he seems absent, silent or turned away.

Sometimes it is judgment. God warns Israel that when they turn to other gods, "I will forsake them and hide my face from them" (Deuteronomy 31:17). The prophets say the same: "your sins have hidden his face from you so that he does not hear" (Isaiah 59:2); "he will hide his face from them at that time, because they have made their deeds evil" (Micah 3:4). The exile is explained in these words: "I hid my face from them and gave them into the hand of their adversaries" (Ezekiel 39:23).

Sometimes it is felt without any reason given, and it is the hardest thing a believer prays about:

  • "How long, O Lord? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me?" (Psalm 13:1)
  • "Why do you hide your face? Why do you forget our affliction and oppression?" (Psalm 44:24)
  • "O Lord, why do you cast my soul away? Why do you hide your face from me?" (Psalm 88:14)
  • "Why do you hide your face and count me as your enemy?" (Job 13:24)

Even here, God is still addressed. The psalmist complains to the God who seems hidden, not about him to someone else. Isaiah waits for him: "I will wait for the Lord, who is hiding his face from the house of Jacob, and I will hope in him" (Isaiah 8:17).

The hiding is not the last word. "In overflowing anger for a moment I hid my face from you, but with everlasting love I will have compassion on you" (Isaiah 54:8). And Ezekiel ends the account of the exile with a promise that ties the face to the Spirit: "I will not hide my face anymore from them, when I pour out my Spirit upon the house of Israel" (Ezekiel 39:29).

One verse asks for the hiding as a mercy: "Hide your face from my sins, and blot out all my iniquities" (Psalm 51:9). The face that must not be hidden from the person can be turned away from the sin.

A face set against

God's face can also be turned toward someone in judgment. "I will set my face against that person and will cut him off from among his people" (Leviticus 20:6). "The face of the Lord is against those who do evil" (Psalm 34:16), a verse Peter quotes unchanged: "the face of the Lord is against those who do evil" (1 Peter 3:12). God's presence is never neutral. To be in it, turned toward, is life. To have it set against you is the end of all safety.

A face sought

Because the face can shine or be hidden, God's people are told to seek it. "You have said, 'Seek my face.' My heart says to you, 'Your face, Lord, do I seek.' Hide not your face from me" (Psalm 27:8–9). Seeking is the people's part when they have turned away: "if my people who are called by my name humble themselves, and pray and seek my face and turn from their wicked ways, then I will hear from heaven and will forgive their sin and heal their land" (2 Chronicles 7:14). God waits for it: "I will return again to my place, until they acknowledge their guilt and seek my face" (Hosea 5:15). It marks out a whole generation: "Such is the generation of those who seek him, who seek the face of the God of Jacob" (Psalm 24:6).

A face beheld

Beyond all of this lies a hope. "The upright shall behold his face" (Psalm 11:7). "As for me, I shall behold your face in righteousness; when I awake, I shall be satisfied with your likeness" (Psalm 17:15). The New Testament takes up the same hope and fulfils it in two stages. Now, God has shone "the light of the knowledge of the glory of God in the face of Jesus Christ" (2 Corinthians 4:6). Then, "we see in a mirror dimly, but then face to face" (1 Corinthians 13:12), and "They will see his face, and his name will be on their foreheads" (Revelation 22:4). Chapters 9 and 11 return to this.
