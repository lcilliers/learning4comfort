3. Comparative Study: The Role of Blood in Major Religions

This document explores the role of blood in major world religions, highlighting similarities and differences, and emphasizing the unique theological significance of blood in Judaism and Christianity.

I. Judaism

Blood is central in sacrificial systems and covenant theology. Key texts: Leviticus 17:11, Exodus 24:8. Blood signifies life and is required for atonement. Prohibition against consuming blood is strict.

II. Christianity

Blood is foundational for redemption and the new covenant through Jesus Christ. Key texts: Hebrews 9:22, Luke 22:20. Unlike Judaism’s repeated sacrifices, Christianity teaches a once-for-all sacrifice of Christ’s blood.

III. Islam

Blood is mentioned in the Qur’an mainly in dietary laws (prohibition of consuming blood). Sacrificial practices exist (Eid al-Adha), but the Qur’an emphasizes that piety, not blood, reaches God (Qur’an 22:37).

IV. Hinduism

Mainstream Hinduism avoids blood due to purity laws and vegetarian ideals. Some folk and Tantric traditions historically used blood offerings, but these are not central to Hindu theology.

V. Ancient Religions

Greek, Roman, and Aztec religions used blood sacrifices to appease gods or secure fertility. Blood was seen as a life-force offered to deities, but without the covenantal or atoning significance found in Judaism and Christianity.

VII. Uniqueness of Judaism and Christianity

Judaism and Christianity share a deep theological link regarding blood: both see blood as essential for covenant and atonement. Christianity builds on Judaism’s foundation but teaches that Christ’s blood fulfills and surpasses the old sacrificial system, offering a once-for-all redemption.
