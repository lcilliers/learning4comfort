I. Introduction

Purpose of the Study: Understand why blood is central to biblical theology and Christian faith.

Key Question: Why does Scripture emphasize blood in relation to life, covenant, and redemption?

II. Foundational Principle

Leviticus 17:11 – 'For the life of the flesh is in the blood…' Blood represents life, and life belongs to God.

Implication: Blood is sacred because it symbolizes life and the cost of sin.

III. Blood in the Old Testament

  1. Blood and Life: Genesis 9:4 – Prohibition against eating blood. Blood = life; life is God’s domain.
  2. Blood and Covenant: Abrahamic Covenant (Genesis 15:9–10, 17–18); Mosaic Covenant (Exodus 24:6–8).
  3. Blood and Sacrifice: Levitical System – Sin offerings (Leviticus 4), Day of Atonement (Leviticus 16). Principle: 'Without shedding of blood there is no forgiveness' (Hebrews 9:22).

IV. Blood in the New Testament

  1. Christ’s Blood and the New Covenant: Luke 22:20; Hebrews 9:12.
  2. Redemption and Justification: Romans 3:25; Ephesians 1:7.
  3. Sanctification and Access: Hebrews 10:19; 1 John 1:7.
  4. Victory and Eternal Life: Revelation 12:11; Revelation 7:14.

V. Theological Themes

  • Atonement: Blood satisfies God’s justice.
  • Covenant: Blood seals divine promises.
  • Purification: Blood cleanses sin.
  • Life and Death: Blood signifies life given for life.

VI. Practical Application

  • Communion: Remembering Christ’s blood.
  • Worship: Gratitude for redemption.
  • Holiness: Living in light of the cost of sin.

VII. Suggested Study Method

  • Word Study: Hebrew 'dam', Greek 'haima'.
  • Cross-References: Trace blood theme through Scripture.
  • Historical Context: Ancient sacrificial practices.
  • Systematic Theology: Read sections on atonement and covenant.

VIII. Reflection Questions

  1. Why is blood necessary for forgiveness?
  2. How does Christ’s blood fulfill Old Testament patterns?
  3. What does the blood of Christ mean for daily Christian living?
